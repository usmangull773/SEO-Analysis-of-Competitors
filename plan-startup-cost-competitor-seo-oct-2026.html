01

What we found

This is a surface review of the four live sites, done before any paid tool access. It tells us where to dig. Each point is labelled Verified when it comes straight from the live site, or Hypothesis when it still needs historical data to confirm. That separation runs through the final report too.

Finding 1: growth is built on page templates, not articles High impact

Verified HowMuchToStart runs one template per business type (101 types) and links each one to 50 state versions, for example /auto-repair-shop/california. Every state page reuses the same cost table with a state multiplier, plus the LLC fee and sales tax. StartCosts lists 53 business types across all 50 states. BizMetricsHQ says it has 646 data pages across 29 industries, and its sitemap lists 355 URLs.

Hypothesis Most of their ranking keywords are long tail queries like "cost to start an auto repair shop in Texas". Ahrefs page level data will confirm which templates carry the traffic.

101 business typesx50 states=5,000+ indexable pages+6 calculators

Finding 3: trust signals are doing ranking work Medium impact

Verified All four publish methodology, editorial or data source pages and put a "last reviewed" date on templates. BizMetricsHQ shows sample sizes on every benchmark card. In a finance adjacent niche this is likely what lets thin, templated pages hold rankings through core updates.

What gets hit in updates
Templated pages with identical copy, no sources, no date and no author or method page.
What these sites do
Unique numbers per page, cited sources (SBA, BLS, state offices), dated reviews and a published method.

Hypothesis StartCosts returns almost no HTML before JavaScript runs. If Google renders it late, some of its pages may be under-indexed, and that gap is worth testing.

1

Rebuild each site's growth history

Pull organic traffic, keyword and referring domain history from Ahrefs and Semrush. Match each traffic jump against Wayback snapshots, sitemap size and Google update dates.

Why: this is how a "growth period" becomes a dated event with a likely cause.

2

Trace every important backlink to its source

Sort links by first seen date, referring domain strength and anchor text. Tag each one as outreach, guest post, data citation, directory, PR or tool embed, with source URLs.

Why: data sites like these often earn links when journalists and bloggers cite a number. That can be copied.

3

Map the templates and keyword clusters that carry traffic

Group top pages by template (business, business plus state, calculator, comparison, guide) and show traffic share, keyword count and internal link depth for each.

Why: it tells you which template to build first and how many pages it needs.

4

Compare all four and split evidence from guesswork

One comparison table across launch date, page count, link velocity, templates and monetisation. Every claim is tagged Verified or Hypothesis.

Why: you asked for data backed conclusions, and this keeps the report honest.

5

Hand over a prioritised roadmap for your site

Step by step build order: data model, page templates, internal linking, trust pages, link earning assets, and what to skip. Each step is ranked by effort against expected impact.

Why: the research is only useful if it turns into a sequence you can execute.
